## Design Specificity Verdict

App Waypoint feels authored rather than category-interchangeable. The buoy artwork, warm paper/deep-water palette, restrained beacon blue, editorial serif, and weekly-issue rhythm create a recognizable publication. The strongest product-specific moment is the homepage hero. The directory pages become more generic below their headings: familiar card grids and utility popovers do their jobs, but do not carry as much of the harbor/editorial character.

The deterministic detector returned zero findings across `src/pages`. Five representative preview pages showed the same 1160px centered shell, no horizontal overflow, a consistent 84px header, and no console errors. It also confirmed locked defining filters and top-aligned app-detail metadata. No reliable user-visible overlay is available because the browser interface did not permit the required mutable script-injection preflight.

## Overall Impression

This is a strong, coherent foundation that now behaves like one product. The biggest opportunity is discoverability: the design is visually calm, but some of its most important navigation and taxonomy affordances are so quiet that readers must already understand them.

## What's Working

- The “Quiet harbor editorial” direction is real in the implementation, especially in the homepage hero, palette, serif/sans split, and measured blue accent.
- Shared shells and repeatable card structure now make app, tag, collection, archive, and issue pages feel related; browser geometry confirmed the alignment.
- The directory controls prevent context-breaking errors: the defining tag or collection is visibly checked and disabled, while optional filters remain available.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Primary navigation is too cryptic

**Why it matters:** Search, archive, and theme are represented only by icons. Tooltips/ARIA labels help assistive technology and hover users, but first-time and touch users must infer the archive-box metaphor. The publication's two central discovery paths—search and past issues—should not depend on icon literacy.

**Fix:** Preserve the compact header, but expose short visible labels at least on desktop, or add a compact labeled navigation treatment that makes “Search” and “Issues” immediately recognizable. Keep the theme toggle icon-only if desired because its state is conventional and secondary.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P1] Directory state is ephemeral and unshareable

**Why it matters:** Compound filters and sort order disappear on refresh/back navigation and cannot be bookmarked or shared. For an experienced audience using the archive as a research tool, this weakens efficiency and confidence.

**Fix:** Encode optional tag/collection filters and sort order in query parameters, restore them on load, and preserve the locked page-defining filter. Keep the current immediate filtering behavior and live count.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P2] Directory pages lose product character below the hero

**Why it matters:** The page heading is highly editorial, but the remainder resolves into a familiar toolbar and uniform card grid. Long directory scans feel more like a generic catalog than a curated waypoint.

**Fix:** Add one restrained, product-specific orientation cue—such as a subtle route line, editorial context strip, or stronger typographic transition between title, count/controls, and grid. Do not add badges or decorative clutter.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable delight`

### [P2] Repeated card metadata makes long grids monotonous

**Why it matters:** Every card carries a title, description, “Best for,” tags, and source with nearly identical weight. The consistency is useful, but across a long grid it becomes tiring and weakens rapid comparison.

**Fix:** Strengthen the scan order by reducing secondary metadata prominence and tightening its rhythm. Preserve every editorial field, but make title, description, and “Best for” read as three clear layers rather than one continuous block.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

### [P2] Large directory titles consume disproportionate mobile attention

**Why it matters:** The expressive serif heading works on desktop, but on smaller screens it can push the app count and controls below the initial viewport, delaying the primary browsing task.

**Fix:** Tighten mobile title scale and vertical rhythm for tag/collection/archive pages while preserving the more dramatic app-detail and homepage moments.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (first-time visitor):** The header shows three unlabeled icons; “archive box” is not a universally obvious synonym for past issues. Jordan can read the current issue but may not discover the catalog structure or broader tag taxonomy.

**Alex (experienced Mac power user):** Search and compound filtering are useful, but a carefully filtered/sorted view cannot be bookmarked, shared, or restored. Repeating the setup is avoidable friction.

**Sam (keyboard/screen-reader user):** Source shows useful labels, `aria-expanded`, live app counts, focus styles, dialog semantics, and disabled contextual filters. The remaining risk is custom listbox/menu keyboard behavior and focus restoration; that needs a dedicated accessibility audit rather than visual inference.

**Casey (distracted mobile reader):** The compact header is touch-friendly, but large directory headings delay controls and results. Icon-only navigation also removes the hover hints Casey would get on desktop.

## Minor Observations

- The filter note “Apps must match every selected filter” is clear but reads as implementation logic; “Show apps matching all selected filters” is slightly more direct.
- The archive rows are intentionally quiet, but stronger differentiation of issue date versus editorial summary would improve rapid scanning.
- The detector found no generic frontend anti-patterns in page markup; remaining issues are product-level judgment, not lint failures.
- Optional-filter auto-close/clear behavior was not dynamically reconfirmed because the browser could not reliably interact with the hidden checkbox; source matches the requested rule.

## Questions to Consider

- Should App Waypoint optimize its header first for visual restraint or for immediate discovery of Search and Issues?
- Is the directory primarily a casual browsing surface or a repeat-use research tool? Persistent URLs matter much more in the latter case.
- How much of the homepage's harbor character should carry into directories before it stops feeling editorially restrained?
