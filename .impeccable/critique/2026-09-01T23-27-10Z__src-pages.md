## Design Specificity Verdict

The front of this page could not belong to another product. The new back of it could belong to almost any of them.

The hero is authored: a lit buoy wordmark, a drawn wave rule, a star field measured off the sea rather than off a fraction of the hero, counter-travelling swell components tuned so crests build in place, and a pick card lit by the featured app's own normalised colour. Nothing about it is transferable.

The Keep Exploring closer is a different animal. Strip the six category names and what remains is the stock related-links block that ships with every docs site and SaaS blog built this decade. It has one authored gesture, the buoy chain, and that gesture is genuinely on-metaphor: a chain of waypoints behind you is what an archive is.

But the specificity is applied to the wrong objects. The two issues that get buoys are the two the card ranks lowest. The featured issue, the one the card is actually recommending, is not on the chain at all, so the route back through the archive is drawn with its first waypoint missing. The catalogue card, half the composition, has no product character whatsoever.

## Overall Impression

The reading spine is in good shape and the engineering underneath it is honest. Assessment B found zero markup findings across pages, components and layouts; zero contrast failures in either theme across 7 to 18 distinct combinations per page; zero horizontal overflow in 21 page/viewport combinations; zero images missing alt; zero focusable elements inside aria-hidden containers; and zero console errors or failed requests across eight routes. That is a genuinely clean technical floor and it is unusual.

The problems are structural and they cluster at the end of the page, which is exactly where today's work landed. The closer answers "where else can I click" when a reader who has just finished an issue is asking "when does the next one arrive". It duplicates the footer 88px below it, offers a strictly worse version of the `/explore/` page it links to, and contains no way to subscribe to a publication whose entire proposition is a Friday cadence.

Two defects in it are outright bugs rather than judgment calls, and both were introduced today.

## What's Working

- The hero atmosphere is exceptional and honestly engineered. Crests hold position and beat against each other, the still level is the high-water mark so the band never clips, each band paints only while on screen, and the loop stops on tab hide.
- Reduced-motion and coarse-pointer discipline is systematic rather than spot-checked: every animated element has a `prefers-reduced-motion` branch in both CSS and JS.
- The closer's card-height balancing is real, not a rationalisation: measured 349px against 349px at desktop, as documented.
- The technical floor listed under Overall Impression. Both assessments independently confirmed the contrast numbers.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The issue has no ending, only a menu

The closer duplicates the footer sitting 88px below it: "Browse archives" against "Archive", "Explore all apps" against "Explore Apps" — the same two destinations under four labels in one screen. The right-hand card is a table of contents for `/explore/`, which is itself a table of contents presenting the same six categories with three sample apps each, so the closer offers a strictly worse version of the page it links to.

This is the last impression of a weekly publication and it currently reads as "you have reached the bottom of a website". The one action that converts a satisfied reader into a returning one is absent: there is no subscribe affordance anywhere in the closer, and the Friday cadence is stated once, 5,400px away in the hero.

Fix: make the closer answer what happens next before where else to click. Put the cadence and a subscribe trigger in it (the `[data-rss-open]` handler is already wired), and either drop the six category links in favour of `/categories/`, which carries a description and count per category, or differentiate the labels from the footer's.

### [P1] "View past issues" shows future issues

The component selects the three newest issues that are not the current one, unconditionally. On the oldest issue page that yields Issues 08, 07 and 06 under a heading reading "View past issues". It is false on 6 of the 8 issue pages. There is also no prev/next issue navigation anywhere, so a reader who lands on Issue 01 cannot read forward without going to the archive.

Fix: minimally, make the heading conditional. Better: on an archived page lead with the next issue in sequence, which fixes the bug and gives the archive the sequential navigation it entirely lacks.

### [P1] The featured entry's grouping inverts on every touch device

The entry uses a 10px gap to bind the issue line to its dek and a 22px margin to separate that group from the buoy chain. Under `pointer: coarse` the issue line takes `min-height: 44px`, inflating its line box. Measured at 375px: the gap inside the group becomes 33px while the gap between groups stays 22px. Proximity is reversed.

This matters because the design's stated grouping mechanism for that card is the spacing ratio, which is why it carries no internal hairline. On every phone and tablet that ratio is backwards, so the card has no grouping mechanism at all.

Fix: buy the 44px target with an expanded hit area rather than `min-height` on an element inside a spacing rhythm, or raise both the gap and the margin together under coarse pointer so the ratio survives.

### [P1] The site ships no 404 page

`src/pages/404.astro` does not exist. The last five commits are tag retirements and route redirects; the URL surface is actively churning and `netlify.toml` carries a hand-maintained redirect list. Any missed redirect drops a reader onto Netlify's generic grey page with no branding, no search, no nav and no way back.

Fix: add `src/pages/404.astro` on the shared page shell with three routes out: current issue, archive, search.

### [P2] The closer inverts type hierarchy and contradicts /archive/

Measured: the card h3 "View past issues" is 30.4px display serif; the featured issue's own title "Issue 07" is 13.44px system sans; its dek is 16px. So the issue title is smaller than its own dek, against the documented Dek rule, and the card's largest type is spent on a navigational label carrying no information. The same object on `/archive/` is 1.38rem display serif — two incompatible treatments of one content object.

Fix: drop the h3s to label rank, since the section h2 already says "Past issues and the full catalog" and they are pure restatement, and promote the featured issue line to title rank so it matches the archive.

### [P2] Homepage heading outline skips h1 to h3 above 921px

Detector-caught, desktop-only. `h2.home-hero-pick-title` is `display: none` at desktop and only `display: block` below 921px, so the homepage outline reads h1 then h3 at 1440 and 1189, and is correct at 375 and 900. A WCAG 1.3.1 break that disappears on mobile, which is why manual review missed it.

Fix: hide it with the `sr-only` pattern already used elsewhere in the codebase rather than `display: none`.

## Persona Red Flags

**Screen reader / keyboard:** 132 focusable elements on the issue page and no skip link. Worse, before the timeline observer fires, both archive links in the closer are focusable at full size with their label content at `opacity: 0` — tabbing into the chain before scrolling puts a focus ring around an empty box for up to 1.7 seconds.

**Phone, interrupted:** the closer is 1,209px tall at 375px, one and a half screens of link list after a long read. The desktop height balancing buys nothing stacked. The likeliest intent, "remind me next Friday", has no answer in the thumb zone or anywhere else.

**The project's own reader** (experienced Mac user, arrives from r/macapps, reads one issue, decides whether to return): lands on Issue 04, reads it, is told to "View past issues" and shown three newer ones, is never offered Issue 05, clicks an app title expecting the App Waypoint page and is thrown to a third-party site in a new tab, then meets "Explore all apps" and "Explore Apps" and wonders if they differ. She leaves without ever meeting the word subscribe outside a small grey footer button. This is the reader the product is written for, and the page loses her at the moment it has earned her.

## Minor Observations

- App cards have two destinations and no tell: title goes to the external homepage in a new tab, the rest of the card goes to the internal detail page. 20 new-tab links per issue page, unannounced.
- `/categories/` is orphaned — nothing links to it, and it is the only page carrying the per-category descriptions and counts the closer withholds.
- `/apps/` is nearly orphaned, reachable only from a carousel link labelled "Open the full directory" and titled "Browse All Apps", colliding with two other Explore labels.
- No `/tags/` or `/collections/` index exists; every tag and collection page is a leaf with no parent.
- Counts doctrine contradicts itself: DESIGN.md argues per-category counts are untrustworthy and must not appear in the closer, while `categories/index.astro` prints them for the same six categories.
- DESIGN.md is drifting from the code: it documents a hero "Start Reading" CTA that no longer exists, a desktop header that no longer matches, and says 28px where the CSS says 22px.
- `.issue-outro { margin-bottom: 88px }` is a hand-set value outside the spacing scale, in a file otherwise rigorous about tokens.
- Reduced-motion is read once at script execution for the timeline; toggling the OS setting after load leaves the chain on its animated path. The wave loop handles this correctly and the closer should match.
- No print stylesheet. An editorial publication is a plausible thing to save as PDF.
- Detector style signals, none of which are failures: tag chips trip a desaturated-gray-on-tinted-surface rule 543 times on `/explore/` at a measured 7.61:1, app card titles sit at 1.15 line-height against a 1.3 guideline, and one Explore link sets 30-plus characters in all caps.

## False Positives Dismissed

- Two `design-system-radius` advisories at global.css:527 and :715. DESIGN.md's Shapes section names 11px and 13px explicitly as the app icon frame exception at roughly 22 to 25 percent of the icon's side. The detector's scale does not encode the documented exception.
- Two interactive targets measured under 24px. The Editor's Pick icon link is grown to exactly 24x24 by an `::after`, confirmed by hit-testing at the corners; the other is an inline link inside a sentence, exempt under WCAG 2.5.8.
- Coarse-pointer counts under 44px are measured against AAA 2.5.5, not the AA 24px bar, which everything passes.
- One intermediate run reporting roughly 42 low-contrast findings was retracted by Assessment B itself: it flipped `data-theme` at runtime without reloading and read stale load-time backgrounds. A proper reload returns zero.
